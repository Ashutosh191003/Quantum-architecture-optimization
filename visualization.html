⚛️
What is a Qubit?
Each dot is a qubit — a tiny quantum memory that can exist in superposition. Lines connecting dots are coupling edges: only connected qubits can exchange quantum information directly.
🔗
Why Connections Matter
To run a quantum program, qubit states must travel along connections. If two qubits that need to interact aren't directly linked, the computer must reroute through intermediate nodes — adding extra SWAP gates.
⚠️
The Cost of Extra Hops
Extra hops = extra gates = more errors & noise. IBM's 20-qubit square lattice is general-purpose — optimised for nobody in particular. For QPE, only 9 of 20 qubits are used, leaving most connections wasted.
🎯
Our Goal
Design an architecture tailored to the specific circuit — minimising the number of SWAP gates while keeping qubit frequencies from colliding with each other.
Step 02
What We Measure
Two complementary metrics define architecture quality
🔁
SWAP Gate Count
Lower is better ↓
When qubits that must interact are not directly connected, the compiler inserts SWAP gates to physically move the quantum state to a neighbouring qubit. Each SWAP is expensive: it takes time, consumes energy, and introduces decoherence noise. Fewer SWAPs = faster, more accurate computation.
TARGET: Minimise Σ d(i,j) across all gate pairs
📡
Yield Rate
Higher is better ↑
Each qubit vibrates at its own resonance frequency. If two neighbouring qubits have similar frequencies, they accidentally interfere — a frequency collision. Yield Rate is the probability that no collision occurs. A yield of 0 means collisions are certain; 1.0 means the design is perfectly collision-free.
TARGET: Maximise P(no collision) via frequency allocation

The same graph-theory principle used in electronics engineering

Quantum ConceptPCB / Electronics Equivalent

Key Insight: Optimising a quantum processor layout is mathematically identical to optimising a PCB — minimise wire length (SWAP gates), avoid signal interference (frequency collisions). Same graph-theory objective function, different physical domain. This is why classical EDA tools and quantum compiler research share algorithms.

Why Molecular Simulation Matters
QPE is the core algorithm for simulating electrons in molecules — essential for drug discovery, battery design, and materials science. A single energy miscalculation cascades into wrong bond predictions, wrong reaction rates, wrong drug candidates. Better processor architecture directly means more trustworthy quantum chemistry.

Real-World Impact
🔐
Post-Quantum Cryptography
Shor's algorithm — which breaks RSA encryption — runs on QPE. Better architecture means faster, more reliable factoring. The race between quantum hardware and post-quantum cryptography standards depends on exactly this kind of optimization.
💊
Drug Discovery
Molecular energy simulation guides the design of new pharmaceuticals. A wrong ground-state energy → wrong binding affinity prediction → wrong drug candidate. Reliable quantum architecture makes quantum-accelerated drug discovery viable.
🔋
Materials & Energy
Designing better batteries, solar cells, and superconductors requires simulating electron behaviour at quantum scale. Trustworthy QPE on optimised hardware is the enabling technology.
